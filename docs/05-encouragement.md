# 05 — Encouragement messages (Stage 5 content, drafted for approval)

Short completion messages shown at the moments the design system allows a "moment" (§4 frequency
gate): section complete (quiet, text only), observation complete (success check + confetti),
calibration complete, weekly target reached. Never during scoring, never per item.

## The evidence, briefly

- **Competence feedback beats generic praise.** Self-determination theory (Deci & Ryan; Ryan & Deci
  2017) finds motivation is sustained by feedback that signals competence and progress, not by flattery.
  So: state what was accomplished, concretely.
- **Small wins.** Amabile & Kramer's progress principle (HBR 2011): the single strongest daily
  motivator in demanding knowledge work is visible progress on meaningful work. So: numbers and
  progress framing ("12 of 15 this week"), not exclamation marks.
- **Purpose framing.** Grant (2008, JAP): connecting a task to its beneficiaries measurably increases
  persistence and output. This study exists to improve teaching in Ugandan classrooms — say so,
  occasionally.
- **Praise the work, not the person, and never compare coders.** Process-praise findings (Mueller &
  Dweck 1998) generalize: person-praise and rankings invite gaming and anxiety. Comparisons would also
  cut against calibration culture. No leaderboards, no "fastest coder".
- **Don't over-reward.** The over-justification literature (Deci, Koestner & Ryan 1999 meta-analysis)
  warns that heavy extrinsic reward can crowd out intrinsic motivation. Messages, not points; and the
  design system already caps confetti at twice an hour.
- **Rotate copy.** Repeated identical messages habituate into invisibility; each trigger draws
  randomly from its pool and no message repeats twice in a row.

## Draft copy

**Section complete** (quiet, inline, no motion beyond the standard check):
1. "Context card done — the classroom is on record."
2. "Notes saved. Every timestamp you wrote is evidence."
3. "All eight concepts scored."

**Observation complete** (the full moment):
4. "Observation complete. That's one more lesson the study can learn from."
5. "Done — your notes, card and scores are safely in."
6. "Another classroom carefully observed. This is the data the project runs on."
7. "Complete. Careful work like this is what makes the scores trustworthy."

**Calibration complete:**
8. "Consensus reached and signed. Calibration is what turns two opinions into a measurement."
9. "Calibration closed — you two just made the instrument sharper."

**Weekly target reached** (dashboard, once):
10. "15 of 15 this week. Steady work, week after week, is how 511 lessons get seen."
11. "Weekly set complete — thank you. Ugandan classrooms are better understood because of it."

**Return after a break** (dashboard, quiet):
12. "Welcome back. You're picking up exactly where you left off — nothing was lost."

Rules: never mention speed, never compare to other coders, never show a countdown, and messages 4–9
appear only at their trigger, drawn at random without immediate repeats.
