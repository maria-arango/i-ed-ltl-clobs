### §5-bis Login as built (D1)

- **Sign-in page:** email address + 4-digit PIN, nothing else. The email field lets the browser
  suggest addresses used before on that device, which is effectively "pick your name". It does
  not publish the roster on a public page; a public name list would expose all 19 enumerators'
  names to anyone on the internet.
- **PINs:**
  - Generated by an admin on the Team screen ("Set PIN", shown once to pass on; resettable).
  - Never equal to the person's ODK ID, and never trivial (0000, 1234, 1111, repeated or
    sequential digits).
  - Stored as a salted `scrypt` hash.
  - Enumerators can change their own PIN after signing in.
- **Lockout:**
  - 5 wrong PINs in a row lock the account for 15 minutes, doubling at each further round.
  - 10 wrong PINs in a row lock it until an admin resets the PIN.
  - A per-IP throttle slows anyone trying many accounts.
  - The screen says how many tries are left.
- **Sessions:**
  - End after 2 hours without activity (the editor's autosave counts as activity) and after
    12 hours in all.
  - End immediately on **Sign out**.
  - The header always shows **"Signed in as <full name> · Sign out"**. Submissions and comments
    record the person, time and device type.
- **Admins** use the same sign-in. The existing email-code sign-in stays in the code as a
  fallback for admins (Resend key optional).
- **Standards, stated honestly:** NIST SP 800-63B-4 would require a secret of at least 15
  characters when it is the only factor. A 4-digit PIN is a **deliberate, documented deviation**,
  chosen for usability on shared devices and recorded in ADR 0003. The compensating controls
  are lockout, per-IP throttling, short sessions, HTTPS-only `HttpOnly` cookies, server-side
  access checks on every request, and an audit trail.

## 0. What you will get, in one paragraph

A landing page that everyone starts from. After sign-in, Narratives enumerators see one tab,
**Narratives**: their assigned videos in order, each marked *Not started*, *In progress* or
*Submitted*. Opening one shows the narrative on a white page, like Google Docs, with a small
toolbar: Undo, Redo, Red, Strikethrough, Comment. Comments sit in the right margin next to the
text they are attached to. The three reflection questions from the Word template sit at the top.
A button opens the video in a new tab. Every keystroke is saved on the device at once and on the
server within seconds. A status line always says "Saved", "Saving…" or "Offline – saved on this
device". Submitting makes the narrative read-only, and an admin can reopen it. Admins get a
Narratives dashboard: progress by enumerator and by video, import and assignment controls,
reopen, version history, and Word downloads (single or a .zip). The Word files reproduce the
sample conventions: red additions, struck text, and real Word comments. The existing scoring
screens move under a **Scoring** section that only admins and Scoring-team accounts can reach.
Nothing in the scoring code, routes or data is deleted.

### 1.2 Review mode — WITHDRAWN by decision D3 (kept for the record)

The template says "keep the original AI text", but in a browser editor one stray Backspace
deletes it. I propose a **review mode that is always on for enumerators**. It does what the
14 sample documents show reviewers doing by hand:

- **New text is red automatically.** Typing, pasting and autocorrect all produce red text. No
  need to pick a colour first.
- **Deleting original (black or grey) text strikes it through instead of removing it.** Selecting a
  wrong phrase and typing the correction leaves the old phrase struck and the new one red right
  after it. This is exactly the dominant pattern in the samples.
- **Deleting your own red text really deletes it**, so typos can be fixed normally.
- The Red and Strikethrough buttons still work manually. A reviewer can colour a struck original
  red (one reviewer's style), un-strike something struck by mistake, or strike their own red
  addition.
- Joining two original paragraphs (Backspace at the start of a paragraph) is blocked. Episode
  structure stays intact.

Implementation: a ProseMirror plugin (~200 lines) that rewrites deleting transactions before they
are applied, plus a rule that adds the red mark to inserted text. It is unit-tested on its own,
including undo/redo and Android keyboard composition.

The dashboard also shows an integrity count per narrative: **original characters removed**
(expected: 0). Admins can see whether anything slipped through.

### 1.3 Layout and accessibility

- A white page (about 816 px wide, the width of a Letter page) on a light neutral grey
  background. Toolbar fixed at the top of the page. Comment cards in a right-margin column
  aligned with their highlighted text. When two cards collide, the lower one is pushed down, as
  in Google Docs.
- Below about 1,100 px wide (tablets), comments move to a slide-over panel opened from the
  toolbar or by tapping highlighted text. Phones get a read-only view with a note to use a
  laptop or tablet (open question Q10).
- Keyboard: Ctrl/⌘+Z and Ctrl/⌘+Shift+Z for undo/redo, Ctrl/⌘+Shift+X for strikethrough,
  Ctrl/⌘+Alt+M for a comment (the Google Docs shortcut), Ctrl/⌘+Shift+R for red. All toolbar
  buttons are reachable by Tab, have visible focus and announce their state (`aria-pressed`).
- **Contrast:** pure `#FF0000` on white is 4.0:1, which fails WCAG AA for body text. On screen,
  red edits render as `#C40000` (about 6:1). The Word export writes exactly `FF0000`, so the
  files match the samples.
- No motion in the editor. Focus moves and status changes are instant.

### 2.3 Access rules (server-side, each with a test)

- An enumerator can read and write **only their own active assignments**. Any other assignment ID
  returns 404, so its existence is not revealed.
- Enumerator responses never contain `lesson_id`, `source_text` or other people's names. This is
  checked with sentinel strings, as in `tests/blinding.test.ts`.
- Enumerator-facing narrative code connects through the **restricted database role** with
  **column-level grants** that exclude `lesson_id` and `source_text`. This is the pattern already
  used for `videos.is_gold`.
- Narratives-only accounts get 403 from every `/api/coder/*` scoring route and are redirected away
  from every scoring page. Scoring-only accounts get 403 from `/api/narratives/*`. These are tested
  at the API layer, not by hiding menus (CLAUDE.md §2).
- No write is accepted for a `submitted` assignment, whatever the client sends. This is enforced in
  the API and again by the trigger.

## 3. Live saving and sync

Goal: no keystroke is ever lost. Three layers protect the work:

1. The device (IndexedDB).
2. The server working copy.
3. The server version history.

### 3.1 On the device

- **Every change is written to IndexedDB within about 250 ms** (trailing debounce, 1 s maximum
  wait). IndexedDB survives reloads, crashes and dead batteries, and holds far more than
  `localStorage`. The draft is also flushed on `visibilitychange` (tab hidden, phone locked) and
  `pagehide`.
- The draft key is **`<user id>:<assignment id>`**, so two people sharing a browser never see each
  other's drafts. The record holds `doc`, `answers`, `comments`, `baseRevision` (the server revision
  it was built on), `localSeq` (a counter per change), `dirty` and `savedAt`. The previous record is
  kept as a backup in case a write is interrupted.
- **`navigator.storage.persist()`** is requested the first time the editor opens. If the browser
  refuses, everything still works but eviction is possible under storage pressure. The result is
  sent with the heartbeat so the dashboard can show it.
- Small wrapper: `idb` (ISC license, about 1 KB).

### 3.2 To the server

- While the draft is dirty, the browser is online and the user is signed in, a save runs 2 s
  after typing pauses, and at least every 10 s during continuous typing.
- `PUT /api/narratives/[assignmentId]/document` carries `{ baseRevision, clientId, doc, answers,
  comments }`. The body is gzip-compressed with the browser's `CompressionStream`: a 100 KB
  narrative becomes about 15–25 KB. Browsers without it send plain JSON.
- The server checks ownership and status, validates `doc` against the same editor schema, and
  saves only if nothing changed in between:
  `UPDATE … SET …, revision = revision + 1 WHERE assignment_id = $1 AND revision = $2`.
  - Success returns the new revision. The device marks the draft clean only if no edits happened
    while the request was in flight.
  - **409 Conflict:** the server copy moved on (another tab or device). See 3.4.
  - **401:** the session expired. See 3.3.
  - **423:** the narrative was submitted. The editor turns read-only and the local copy is kept.
  - **Network error or 5xx:** status "Offline – saved on this device". Retry with exponential
    backoff (2 s doubling up to 60 s, with jitter), and immediately on the browser's `online` event.
- **Version history:** a save also writes a `narrative_revisions` snapshot when the last snapshot
  is more than 3 minutes old, **when the text shrank by more than 200 characters or 2%** (a
  possible accidental deletion), when a comment was removed, and always on submit, reopen,
  restore and conflict. A two-hour review produces at most a few dozen snapshots. Postgres
  compresses them automatically.
- **Status line** (always visible, announced politely to screen readers): *Saved* · *Saving…* ·
  *Offline – saved on this device* · *Signed out – saved on this device* · *Opened in another
  tab* · *Needs your choice* (conflict) · *Submitted – read-only*.

### 3.3 Session expiry

- A 401 never discards anything. The editor stays on screen and the draft stays in IndexedDB. A
  dialog says "You were signed out. Your work is saved on this device." and offers **Sign in
  again**, which opens in a new tab so the editor keeps its state.
- After sign-in (detected through a `BroadcastChannel` message or on focus), syncing resumes and
  the pending draft goes up.
- If the person instead reloads, or signs in on the same device the next day, loading the
  narrative compares the device draft with the server copy:
  - **Device draft is dirty and built on the current server revision:** it is the newer work, so
    it is loaded and saved straight away. This covers crash, battery, closed tab and session
    expiry.
  - **Device draft is clean, or there is none:** the server copy is loaded.
  - **Device draft is dirty but the server has moved on:** this is a conflict (3.4).
- **Signing out with unsaved changes** shows "Your latest changes have not reached the server yet.
  Connect to the internet, or sign out anyway (they stay on this device until you return)."
  After a fully synced sign-out, that person's drafts are removed from the device. This matters
  on shared devices.

### 3.4 Two tabs or two devices: never overwrite silently

- **Same device, two tabs:** the Web Locks API (`navigator.locks`) lets exactly one tab hold the
  edit lock for a narrative. A second tab opens read-only with "This narrative is open in another
  tab — **Edit here instead**". Taking over moves the lock; the first tab saves what it has and
  turns read-only. If Web Locks is unavailable, a `BroadcastChannel` heartbeat does the same job.
- **Two devices** (or anything that slips past the lock): the revision check refuses the stale
  save with 409. The refused version is **stored on the server as a `conflict_copy` revision**,
  so it is never lost. The person then sees both versions with their times ("This device, 14:02"
  / "Saved from another device, 14:10") and picks which one continues. The other stays in
  history, and an admin can restore it.

### 3.5 Tests for the failure cases you listed

All of these run in CI. The sync engine is a plain TypeScript module with its storage, network
and clock injected, so it can be tested without a browser.

## 5. Login: original recommendation — SUPERSEDED by D1 / §5-bis (kept for the record)

How sensitive the data is: narratives quote classroom speech and describe minors' appearance and
behaviour. That is Level 3–equivalent. Accounts must be personal, because submissions and
comments are attributed. Many devices are shared.

What NIST SP 800-63B-4 (August 2025) says, quoted from the published text:

- A password used **on its own** must be **at least 15 characters** (§3.1.1.2). A 4-digit code
  on its own does not meet NIST in any reading.
- A PIN **may** be all digits when it is an **activation secret** that unlocks something the
  person has (a device-bound authenticator). It must be at least 4 characters (6 recommended), and
  the authenticator must be **disabled after no more than 10 consecutive failures**, with a
  different authenticator then required (§3.2.10).
- **Email "SHALL NOT be used for out-of-band authentication"** (§3.1.3.2). Email codes are
  acceptable for **confirming an email address or recovery**, not as the login factor. So
  today's email-code login is itself outside NIST's list of authenticators, even though it is
  common practice.
- Reauthentication should happen at least every 30 days (AAL1) or 24 hours with a 1-hour idle
  timeout (AAL2). "Remember my browser" cookies "SHALL NOT be used instead of authentication"
  (§2, §5.1).
- Several people may register the same device (§3: services "SHOULD NOT prevent a device from
  being registered as an authenticator by multiple subscribers").

### 5.1 Recommendation: C, device enrolment + name + PIN

1. **First time on a device:** the enumerator enters an **enrolment code**.
   - By default, an admin generates it on the dashboard ("Give <name> a code": 8 characters,
     valid 24 hours, single use) and hands it over by phone or in person. No email service is
     needed.
   - It can come by email instead, if Harvard's SMTP relay is available (Q13).
   - They then set a personal **4-digit PIN** (first time ever) or enter their existing PIN. A
     blocklist rejects 0000, 1234, 1111 and similar.
   - **Do not reuse the ODK codes.** They are the enumerator IDs, printed on handouts and present
     on every device (STATUS §6.2).
2. **Every day:** the landing page shows **"Who is working?"** with the names enrolled on this
   device. Tap a name, type the PIN, and you are in.
   - The header always shows **"Signed in as <Name> · Not you? Switch person"**.
   - Sessions end after **30 minutes idle**. Getting back in takes only the PIN; the editor
     draft is safe on the device.
   - Sessions also end at **12 hours**, and immediately on **Sign out**.
3. **Lockout:**
   - 5 wrong PINs disable that person's enrolment **on that device**. They need a new enrolment
     code. This is within NIST's limit of 10.
   - 10 failures across all devices in 24 hours lock the account until an admin resets the PIN.
   - Each failure also adds a growing delay.
   - Remaining attempts are shown ("2 tries left"), as NIST §8 advises.
4. **Admins** sign in the same way, and admin-only actions (exports, downloads, assignment,
   account changes) also require re-entering the PIN if the last one was over 1 hour ago.
5. **Storage:**
   - The device secret is a random 256-bit token in an `HttpOnly`, `Secure`, `SameSite=Strict`
     cookie, stored only as a hash on the server.
   - The PIN is hashed with `scrypt` (built into Node) and a per-person salt.
   - Admins can see and revoke each enrolled device on the Team screen.
   - Every submission records person, device and time.

### 5.2 Honest caveats

- A cookie-held device secret is a bearer token. NIST prefers a key that cannot be copied off the
  device. The stronger variant stores a **non-extractable WebCrypto key** in the browser and signs
  a server challenge at each PIN entry. It costs about one more day, and I recommend it if DAT asks
  how "something you have" is enforced.
- On a shared device, someone who learns a colleague's PIN can act as them. That is the same as
  in ODK today. The mitigations are personal PINs never written down, lockout, and an audit trail
  of which device did what.
- The current email-code flow and the `sessions` table stay in the codebase, unused, if you choose
  C. Nothing is deleted.
