WEB APPLICATION

We are working on the LTL Project, a teacher-training intervention in Uganda that looked to improve teaching practices and learning, we have school-level randomization (3 arms: control, dispersed and connected) and teacher-level randomization (many types of assignment). Post-intervention, classes were recorded in 2024 to see how teacher practices and teaching quality had improved, changed and evolved. Now it’s the time to watch those videos and score them. We have designed a summarized, Classroom-Observations instrument and now wish to advance to a more systematic and efficient way of inputting the coding scores from enumerators. We want to be able to save each enumerators inputted information in a style that is easy to track progress (how many videos each has watched, how scores differ, how far along the 536 videos we are, etc), and that also saves, presents, allows to download the scores in a tidy dataset that can be fed to the AI to train it.

Right now, these are the different places we store information in and how it is stored:

- Video notes - Current place: Google Docs, each coder had a Google Docs file with tabs inside for each teacher they observe - coders take notes live as they watch each video, it’s prose.
- Scores and justifications - Current place: Google Sheets - each coder has two columns, on one they enter their score for that teacher (4 options), in the other the justification for the option they selected - one sheet per video, one row per concept 
- Rubric  - Current place: Overleaf/PDF - 8 pages with each concepts Importance of Concept, Indicator, Anchor Descriptions and Examples
- Context cards - Current place: Google Sheets - one row per teacher describing generalities of the setup of the classroom and camera position so the AI can better identify the setup of the video (uniforms, door location, teacher appearance, room appearance, camera position)

I have uploaded examples of how each of these look currently to these project files so you also have an idea of what needs to be present in the website. I have also attached a list of the 536 videos plus each school’s school-level assignment and specific teacher-assignment.

This is why combining the information to train the AI ended up being complicated. The spirit now is to have an integral platform where all the information that coders enter can be saved in one place. There’s one caveat. The videos contain minors, meaning they’re Level 3 data and should be managed accordingly and with care. Even if only people from the team either at HGSE or Kimanya are the one that will access the videos, it’s still sensitive to upload them to a third party platform, which is why I am thinking it would be best to just link the videos in the platform so the enumerators still watch them from the safe place in Drive, and then all the data inputs are recorded in the application.

The other part is calibration. Enumerators have to be calibrated on theirs scores and on what they observe, the more they calibrate, the more their answers converge towards where the rubric wants them to. After watching the videos, enumerator pairs have to come together and calibrate then reach a final consensus on the score of each item. In the past these consensus were also informal, done on the share spreadsheets and were just highlight in different colors to define the final score agreed on. The platform needs to allow a place that can only be “started” when both enumerators are logged in, where they can easily compare the responses they added and their justifications, and then be able to select a final score. This must also be recorded for us to train the AI, both the intermediate scores and the final ones after calibration.

Requirements for the app:

- There needs to be some type of login point since we are going to have approximately 14 people coding the videos. Each person should only access the videos they have been assigned to.
- Each video has to be watched by 2 different people. Enumerators will work as couples.
- It would be good to randomize the assignment of the videos to ensure equal coverage across schools and school arms as enumerators advance, also assign some type of priority to treated teachers in treated schools so they are watched first. This means the website should have a randomization algorithm to assign videos. The admins can log in and assign the enumerator pairs, but the website would be in charge od randomly assigning the videos. We must make sure every groups gets a wide variety of videos, only watching videos from connected, only from dispersed, only from controls, can greatly bias the scores.
- It is necessary that the coding of each video is done in a seating, enumerators may be moved to watch the video then code just a part of the rubric and return to it later. This will also be introduced when we train enumerators but the platform should be able to keep some kind of trace of how long it takes each enumerator to complete an observations. We’re expecting around 2 hours. I’m thinking about a timer.
- Coders would have no knowledge on from which school is each teacher, or what assignment they have.
- As an admin, me an my team would be able to have an additional dashboard (that only shows up in our profiles) and that keeps track of the overall team advances, for everyone and by working groups (since we’ll be working in couples)
- The platform should also be able to adapt to changes in the team or in availability of the coders. The enumerators we end up choosing will work on this full time, but some of us from the team will work on it 100%, 75% or 50% of the time from varying dates (as there are more projects asides this), so we should be able to regroup people and assigned videos if needed be.
- It’s very important for me that it is easy to navigate the website, that I can move freely, that it has a home icon and return arrows, that it is predictable if I hit the return arrow I will go back to the immediately page I was in before.
- The aesthetics of the app are important. They should follow guidelines on best user experience from leading tech companies and academics and literature evidence that is up to date in 2026. Good flow, rounded borders, excellent colorimetry, appropriate fonts (work-inducing fonts, that show time was taken to develop the space and boring-office-like fonts that incite boredom and not wanting to work). Smooth live transitions between pages/tabs are important, they improve the experience, I don’t want it to just jump from one other the other (revise latest trends in animation between pages/in a multi-page website - check these resources for many of the examples I’ve liked and have in mind: https://htmlburger.com/blog/web-transitions/ https://orpetron.com/blog/top-10-websites-with-outstanding-page-transition-animations/). If something is loading I want a cool interesting objects that shows its loading and its progress. I want the difference pages and sections to have modern 2026 icons and good and easy click-ability.
- I imagine the website to have light cream colors, maybe mixed with brown, red and blue - might still change along the way.
- Final individual and calibrated scores must be able to be downloaded in a seamless manner. Only admins can download this information. The information must be presented in a tidy manner that is easy to almost immediately feed to the AI.
- Maybe adding images from Kimanya to the website would be good. Enumerators are from Uganda, forming that link to their local context could help motivate them to code and log in. Maybe use the image as a landing page, but if it’s not seemed very necessary then we don’t have to include images.
- I want to host this in GitHub, meaning I don’t want anything to be local from the start. Claude can then run git commands when needed be. The repository also has to be well maintained with a clear READ.ME and any other maintainance documents.

The experience I picture:

- I’m sent a link with my initial credentials. Maybe just a username, as passwords make everything complicated. When I enter, I can see a list of the videos I have been assigned to and the deadlines (one week has 5 days, working full time each person should complete 3 a day and 15 a week, so be clear that by the end of the week they have ti have that ready.), maybe I can see a sneak peak of the next videos I have been assigned. I can also see a dashboard of my progress, how many observations I’ve coded, how many I have left, maybe stats on how long it usually takes me to complete a videos, maybe information/ descriptive statistics on how I often grade overall and each concept (frequency on the 4 option), clean, interactive, smooth to navigate and understand. Then I clink on the video I choose to watch from my set, and I move to a new window where I’m shown the possible places I can go to: a Google Drive link to the video (presented in a nice darker shade rectangles with the tr_id and the link to Google Drive video), notes, scoring & justification, context cards (here’s where it’s tricky for me how to keep track of time spent as the time taken to write the notes and the context card does not matter much, what matters is coding an observation in a single session). Then I can choose to go to the notes and that’s where I’ll write the notes I take while watching the video from Google Drive, when I’m done with that I can move freely to the justifications & scores, or to the context cards. When I am set to enter the scores, I get a timer (I also doubt the timer, if the video ends up being able to be accessed from outside the platform in drive, coders could just choose when to watch it, then records all scores and justification separately not heir own excel file or similar and then just paste all of that into our platform - which makes me think the timer could be perjudicial instead of helpful). So I enter the scores, and while I enter the scores, I’m able to access each definition from the rubric, (this part is very important), I can either get a table where I input each score row by row and the rubric definitions on the left or right side, or go item by item, transitions are very smooth, when I read a concept I get to read the importance of concept, indicators and special note ALWAYS as well as the table with each score, then, when I choose which score, I also get a type of recommendation with the information of that specific score that kind of promos the person to verify if they coded well according to the guidance. I enter everything, hit complete, then can return and go to the contexts cards to fill that information in as well. Important note, I believe it might be less brain-tiring and consuming to indicate the enumerators to fill the contexts cards first, as it helps them get familiar with the video - if they were to fill them afterwards, they’re probably gonna be tired of watching, note-taking, and scoring the video. It also helps them get a bit of context of the environment of the classroom. Getting back to the experience, I hit complete and can see a completion status of each of the parts: notes, scores, context cards 100% completed, meaning it would be good to have a “completion” button on each. Reinforcement and feedback is good, we could drop messages that incentivize the coders, either related to education or that congratulate them on finishing smaller steps. They can hit one final complete button for the whole observation or just return to the homepage, where their dashboards will have updated with their new inputted information, and then they can repeat the process again. Another thing that would improve my experience even more is being able to read my notes side-by-side as I’m creating my scores since the notes are what support the coding and justifications most so being able to access both at the same time would be ideal. Then, after I’ve watched my set of videos and set a time to calibrate with the other coder I was assigned that video to, I meet with them and when we’re both logged in and ready to calibrate (there needs to be some type of live tracking - to make sure both enumerators are in), we “start” calibration of the specific videos we share (ideally one pair should watch as many of the same videos together, however, because of team changes, it is possible they’d have to code with more people additionally), so being able to select whom I want to calibrate with, which video, being on a pre-room and then access the comparisons and calibration space at once (this is fundamental because enumerators shouldn’t be able to access other enumerators’ scoring BEFORE calibration as that can bias them, or move them towards not scoring and just rephrasing the other’s scores in name of working quickly, with less effort). Back to it, when I’m finally in this share space, we enter a place where we can see side by side the scores and justification of the other person and then some setup where we can decide on the final score, both parts should sign on it and hit complete before a calibration is completed. They can then move to the next video they want to calibrate. It would be also good to have some plots or stats on each coder’s dashboard as well as the admin’s (who can see everything - not just their own but all coders) on on average how close is a coder’s individual response to the agreed score overall and for each video - this helps to see whether coders are consistent and remain calibrated. After I’m done I can stay logged in or just log out. It’s very important for me that it is easy to navigate the website, that I can move freely, that it has a home icon and return arrows, that it is predictable if I hit the return arrow I will go back to the immediately page I was in before.
